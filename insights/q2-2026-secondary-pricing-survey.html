Q2 2026 Secondary Pricing Survey: buyout pricing firms as spreads narrow

Published July 2026 · SecondariesMatch Research Desk · 6 min read

Secondaries pricing continued to firm through the second quarter, with buyout fund interests trading at an average of 91% of net asset value — up three points from Q1. The move reflects two forces working in the same direction: a smaller universe of forced or urgent sellers, and buyers redeploying record levels of dry powder raised over the past eighteen months.

Where the discount narrowed fastest

Diversified buyout and large-cap credit vehicles saw the sharpest repricing, as buyers competed for scarce, well-diversified NAV. Venture and growth-equity interests remained the most discounted category, though even there the gap to par has compressed as primary markets have reopened and marks have caught up to secondary reality.

  • Buyout (diversified): 91% of NAV, tightest spread of any strategy
  • Credit / direct lending: 92–96% of NAV, supported by current income
  • Venture & growth equity: 78–86% of NAV, still the widest spread
  • Real assets (infrastructure, real estate): bifurcated — core infrastructure at or above par, office-heavy real estate well below

What buyers are watching into Q3

GP-led continuation vehicle volume is on pace to set a new full-year record, which is pulling buy-side attention — and capital — away from traditional LP-interest strips. Buyers with flexible mandates that can move between LP portfolios and GP-led deals are seeing the widest opportunity set and the best relative pricing, since they aren't forced to compete in a single, increasingly crowded lane.

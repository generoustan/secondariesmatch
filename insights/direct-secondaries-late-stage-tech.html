Direct secondaries in late-stage tech: the discount is closing

Published May 2026 · SecondariesMatch Research Desk · 5 min read

Discounts on direct stakes in late-stage venture and growth-equity companies have compressed meaningfully over the past two quarters. Employees, early investors, and funds nearing the end of their life have been the primary sellers; growth-stage secondary funds, crossover investors, and strategics have been the primary buyers — and the gap between what each side wants to pay has narrowed as primary markets have reopened and repriced the last vintage's marks closer to reality.

Why the discount is shrinking

Three forces are converging. Primary financing rounds are back for the highest-quality late-stage companies, which gives secondary buyers a fresher reference price than a stale prior-round mark. Company-run tender offers have become more common, which both provides price discovery and normalizes secondary liquidity as a routine event rather than a distress signal. And a widening set of dedicated direct-secondaries funds has increased competition for the best names, particularly in AI infrastructure, vertical software, and healthcare technology.

What still trades at a discount

  • Companies without a primary round in the past 18 months, where the reference price is stale
  • Stakes requiring company or board consent with no fast-track precedent
  • Sectors still working through valuation resets — certain consumer and late-stage fintech names

For sellers, the practical implication is that timing and documentation now matter more than they used to: a stake with a recent primary mark, clean cap-table position, and a company with a track record of consenting to transfers will clear closer to that mark than one without.
